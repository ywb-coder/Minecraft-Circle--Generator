A fountain is the fastest way to make a Minecraft plaza look finished. It fills empty space, gives you a reason to use water, and — done right — becomes the visual anchor of your whole base. These seven designs range from a 20-minute starter fountain to a palace centerpiece, and every one of them starts with a circle.

## 1. The Classic Stone Basin

The fountain every player should build first. Lay a **15-block circle** of stone bricks at ground level using our [circle blueprint](/circle/15/), dig the inside 2 blocks deep, and line the rim with stairs facing inward. Place a 3-block pillar of stone in the center with a water source on top. The water flows down the pillar into the basin. Cost: about 150 blocks and 20 minutes.

## 2. The Three-Tier Wedding Cake

Stack three shrinking circles — 15, 11, and 7 — with 2 blocks of vertical spacing between tiers. Put water sources on the top tier so they cascade down the sides. Every tier rim gets stairs for the overflow edge. Build each tier as a solid disc, then dig 1 block deep channels for water. The cascade hides most imperfections, which makes this the most forgiving design on the list.

## 3. The Glowstone Night Fountain

Take the classic basin and light it from below: replace the basin floor with a ring of sea lanterns, then add a single glowstone pillar at the center instead of stone. At night the water reads as a glowing blue disc from across the base. Tip: keep the rim in dark stone (deepslate bricks) so the glow has maximum contrast.

## 4. The Corner Wall Fountain

Not all fountains need a plaza. Place a **9-block half-circle** against a wall with the flat side facing the wall, rise the back edge 3 blocks, and let water pour from the top lip into a small pool below. This is the smallest design on the list — under 80 blocks — and perfect for hallway ends and castle courtyards.

## 5. The Sunken Plaza Fountain

Dig a 25-block circle 4 blocks deep, rim it with white concrete stairs, and put a 7-block-wide fountain in the center. The surrounding plaza floor sits at ground level, so visitors look down into the water. Light the pit with glowstone under the rim. Best paired with a garden build — see our [garden designs guide](/blog/minecraft-garden-designs-with-circles/) for matching beds.

## 6. The Spire Fountain

For a vertical accent, stack five rings of 7, 5, 5, 3, 3 blocks to form a tapered spire in the middle of a 15-block basin. Water sources go at the top and halfway up the spire, creating two separate cascades. Add a second smaller spire beside it for a "matching pair" fountain that fills 2×2 plaza corners nicely.

## 7. The Mega Centerpiece

A 33-block circular pond with a central island holds a statue or an ice sculpture. Ring the pond with alternating white and light-gray concrete in a checkerboard, and place sea lanterns every 4 blocks on the underwater rim. The island is a 9-block circle connected by a 2-block bridge. This design works best as the center of a roundabout or a [mega build](/blog/minecraft-mega-build-ideas/).

## Water Placement Cheat Sheet

All of these designs depend on the same three water rules:

- **One source block is enough.** A single water block at the top of any structure flows downward forever. You rarely need more than 3–5 sources per fountain.
- **Sources, not streams.** Fill basins with source blocks (scoop and re-place with a bucket) so the water sits flat instead of flowing in streams.
- **Underwater lights.** Sea lanterns and glowstone both work under water; torches and lanterns do not. Light your basins from underneath or from the rim.
