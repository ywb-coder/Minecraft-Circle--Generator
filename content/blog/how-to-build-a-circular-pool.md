A circular pool is the classic "quick build that impresses everyone" — it takes about thirty minutes, uses maybe 300 blocks, and instantly upgrades a base's backyard. The hard parts aren't the circle; they're the water. This guide covers the full build: blueprint, digging, lining, filling with source blocks, and lighting so the pool glows instead of spawning mobs.

## Pick the Size and Get the Blueprint

For a first pool, **15 blocks wide**. It's big enough for two players, small enough to finish before dinner. The [circle generator](/?t=circle&d=15) gives you the exact 56-block ring — screenshot it or keep it open while you build.

Clear a 19×19 pad (2 blocks of walkway around the pool on every side) and mark the center with a temporary block. Lay the ring on the surface, then walk the outline to confirm the curve reads correctly before you dig.

## Dig and Line the Basin

**Step 1: Dig to 3 deep.** Remove every block inside the ring down to a depth of 3. The bottom layer is at ground level minus 3, and the walls are vertical for now — the curve comes later.

**Step 2: Line the bottom.** Floor the 3-deep basin with light blue concrete. A 15-block circle's floor runs about 170 blocks. Line the walls one block high with the same concrete so the rim-to-bottom transition is clean.

**Step 3: Add the ledge.** One block from the top of the basin, place a 1-block-wide ledge ring of white concrete stairs around the full circumference — the "sitting ledge" every Minecraft pool needs. This is where the circle really shows: the ledge ring must follow the same curve as the outer ring, one block in.

**Step 4: Build the rim.** Ring the top edge with stone brick stairs facing inward, all the way around. The stairs double as both the pool edge and the walkway edge.

## Fill the Pool: Sources, Not Streams

Here is the step that ruins more pools than any other: water placed from a bucket flows in *streams*, and a basin filled with streams has that ugly rippling texture. Fix it with sources:

- Place a water source block in one corner of the bottom.
- Hold a bucket in your hand and right-click the *air above* the first source, scooping and replacing to multiply sources.

Wait — the real trick is simpler: fill the pool **with a bucket, one block at a time**, moving in a grid. Each block placed is a source block that doesn't flow. A 15-block pool needs about 170 source placements; go in rows of 10 and it takes four minutes. Alternatively, place 4–6 source blocks strategically and let them merge — the merged pool is flat enough that most players never notice.

The professional method for big pools: fill the bottom level with sources only. Water above a source block stays a source when placed by bucket, so one careful pass across the bottom gives you a perfectly still pool.

## Light and Detail

A dark, wet basin is a mob spawner. Three rules:

- **Sea lanterns on the bottom.** Place them in a ring 3 blocks in from the rim, every 4 blocks — about 12 lanterns for a 15-block pool. They light the water and the ledge at night.
- **Glowstone under the rim.** A row of glowstone behind the rim stairs makes the pool edge glow without touching the water.
- **No torches near the water.** Torches put out orange light that ruins the blue; use lanterns on fence posts around the walkway.

## Make It Bigger or Make It Yours

The same build scales to any diameter. A 25-block pool (about 470 perimeter blocks) becomes the centerpiece of a courtyard; a 33-block pool with a 9-block island in the middle turns into a [fountain build](/blog/best-minecraft-fountain-designs/). For an oval pool — the shape that fits along a house wall — use the [oval generator](/?t=circle&d=15) with a width-to-height ratio of about 2:1 and the exact same digging, lining, and filling steps.

Whatever size you choose, the sequence never changes: blueprint, ring, dig, line, ledge, rim, sources, lights. Get the blueprint first and the pool builds itself.
