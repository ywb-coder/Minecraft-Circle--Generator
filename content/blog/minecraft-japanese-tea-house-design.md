The Japanese tea house is the most peaceful build in Minecraft. Small footprint, natural palette, and a layout that rewards patience over block count — the whole structure fits in a 21×21 area and looks incredible from every angle. This guide takes you from a flat clearing to a finished tea house with garden, pond, and porch, in about two hours.

## Site and Layout

Find a quiet spot near cherry trees or bamboo. Clear a 21×21 area and mark the center — the tea house itself is a **9×9 room with a 3-block-wide porch on the south side**, so the center of the structure sits 4 blocks north of the area's center. Everything else — garden, path, pond — fills the remaining space around it.

The north side gets a rock garden, the east side a bamboo clump, and the south side a small koi pond. That one decision — garden north, water south — gives the whole build a natural walking flow.

## Frame and Walls

**Step 1: Lay the foundation.** Place a ring of stripped dark oak logs, 11×11 (room plus porch foundation), on the ground. The dark wood grounds the build visually against the green surroundings.

**Step 2: Raise the corner posts.** Dark oak log posts at the four corners of the 9×9 room, plus two posts at the porch corners, 3 blocks tall.

**Step 3: Fill the walls.** The west, north, and east walls are bamboo planks with white terracotta trim — bamboo planks for the main face, terracotta in a single horizontal band at waist height. The south side stays open except for the porch posts.

**Step 4: Add the shoji window band.** On the north wall, a row of pale oak trapdoors under the eave reads as the traditional sliding door pattern without any mods. Two trapdoor strips per wall section.

## The Roof: The Hardest Part, Made Simple

The roof is a 2-layer slab pyramid. Build a solid 11×11 platform of dark oak slabs with a 1-block overhang on every side, then a second layer one block in from each edge with a 9×9 platform. Add dark oak slabs on the ridge line for the cap. Two-layer slab roofs are quick, read as "sloped" from every distance, and never have the alignment problems of full stair roofs.

Eaves matter more than the roof itself: place cherry leaves under the roofline on all four sides so the transition from dark roof to pale walls is soft.

## Interior: Tatami, Hearth, and Light

Inside the 9×9 room, lay bamboo plank flooring with a 3×3 raised platform of pale oak on the north wall — the tatami and the hearth in one. Place the hearth as a 2×1 pit of lava covered by a slab, or skip the lava entirely and use a campfire for the traditional tea warmth. A lantern hangs from the center beam on a chain.

Keep the interior to four items: the hearth, two wooden slabs for seats, a crafting table tucked in the corner, and a chest. Tea houses look best when they look lived-in, not decorated.

## The Garden: Rock Garden, Path, and Koi Pond

**Rock garden:** on the north side, a 7×7 square of sand with three gravel mounds (2-block-high piles) arranged in a loose triangle. Rake lines are impossible in vanilla — instead, border the garden with a single row of smooth stone and call it done.

**Path:** white concrete powder from the porch to the east, curving around to the garden. A 2-block-wide path with flowers on one side only keeps it quiet.

**Koi pond:** on the south side, a **15×9 oval** — width 15, height 9 — filled 1 block deep with water. See our [oval guide](/blog/how-to-build-an-oval-in-minecraft/) for the outline trick. Line the rim with dark oak slabs and drop two sea lanterns on the bottom. The pond is the screenshot centerpiece, so it deserves the full oval blueprint from the [generator](/?t=circle&d=15).

## Finishing Touches

Three last details complete the scene:

- **Bamboo screen.** A 2-block-tall bamboo wall on the east edge, 3 blocks deep, hides the pond from the path — the classic "reveal" moment when the path turns.
- **Stone lantern.** A 2-block stone pillar with a sea lantern cap at the path's corner. The sea lantern throws a white light that matches the stone instead of the orange of torches.
- **Azalea accents.** Flowering azalea at the porch corners and beside the stone lantern.

Step onto the porch, light the lanterns, and watch the sun set over the pond — the tea house build is done, and it took less than a thousand blocks.
