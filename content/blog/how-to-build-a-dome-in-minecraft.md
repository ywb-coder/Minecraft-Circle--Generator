Domes are the most satisfying roofs in Minecraft. They turn a plain circular building into a statement — observatories, greenhouses, libraries, and grand halls all benefit from a smooth curve overhead. And like everything round in the game, a dome is just the top half of a sphere. Learn the ring method once, and you can roof any round building in an hour.

## The Ring Method: Domes Are Half Spheres

Standing on the ground, a dome looks like a stack of shrinking circles. The bottom ring is the full diameter of your building, and each ring above it is a few blocks narrower until a single block caps the top. For a **15-block dome**, the rings from the ground up are: **15, 13, 11, 7, 3, 1** — six rings rising about 6 blocks above your floor level.

The easy way to think about it: take a full [sphere blueprint](/blog/how-to-build-a-sphere-in-minecraft/) for your diameter, keep only the upper half of the rings, and place the bottom ring at floor level.

## Step-by-Step: A 15-Block Dome

**Step 1: Lay the base circle.** Mark the center of your building and place the full 15-block ring at floor level (about 56 blocks). This ring is also the outline of your interior floor, so make sure the floor is dug out before you continue.

**Step 2: Stack the rings.** Build the 13-block ring directly on top of the base, then the 11-block ring, then 7, then 3, and finish with the single cap block. Every ring shares the same center — count from your center marker, not from the previous ring, and the dome stays perfectly centered.

**Step 3: Frame the doorway.** Cut one block out of the bottom two rings for a 2-block-tall entrance. If you are building an observatory, leave a 2×2 opening facing whatever you want to observe.

**Step 4: Fill the gaps.** Solid domes are one block thick with nothing inside the curve. For a greenhouse look, fill the rings with glass panes instead of solid blocks — place the panes along the ring outline so the frame stays visible from outside.

**Step 5: Detail the rim.** The bottom ring is where domes look unfinished. Add a one-block overhang ledge around the base (a ring of slabs or stairs) to visually tie the roof to the walls below.

For domes on even-diameter buildings, the top becomes a 2-block cap and the base ring sits around a 2×2 center gap — mirror the same even-diameter logic you use for circles.

## Interior Tricks: Lighting, Walls, and Skylights

A dome only looks good when the room under it does. Three upgrades that cost almost nothing:

- **Central pillar of light.** A sea lantern or glowstone block hanging at the exact center of the dome lights the whole room evenly. It also marks your center while you build the rings.
- **Skylight cap.** Swap the single top block for a glass block. Builds with a 2-block cap get a 2×2 glass skylight instead.
- **Corner stairs.** Place stair blocks facing inward on the lowest ring's four cardinal points. It breaks up the flat wall-join line and hides the seam between walls and dome.
