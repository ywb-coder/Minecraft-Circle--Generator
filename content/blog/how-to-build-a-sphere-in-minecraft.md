A Minecraft sphere is the classic flex build — floating islands, death star bases, giant pumpkins, planetary decorations. It is also much easier than it looks, because a sphere is nothing more than a stack of circles. Master the circle, and you already know ninety percent of the sphere. In this guide you will learn the slice method used by every serious builder, with exact ring sizes for popular diameters.

## The Slice Method: A Sphere Is Stacked Circles

Imagine a sphere as horizontal layers, like stacking plates. Every layer is a circle of a different diameter: the middle layers are the widest, and the layers near the top and bottom shrink until they become a single block. Build each circle using the same quadrant technique from our [circle guide](/blog/how-to-build-a-circle-in-minecraft/), and the whole sphere assembles itself.

For a **15-block sphere**, the layer widths from the bottom are: **1, 3, 7, 11, 13, 15, 15, 15, 13, 11, 7, 3, 1**. That is thirteen layers total. Memorize the rhythm — it applies to every sphere size, just with more steps in the middle.

## Step-by-Step: Building a 15-Block Sphere

**Step 1: Choose your center.** Mark the exact center block of the sphere — for a build at `Y: 64`, put a marker block there and count up and down from it.

**Step 2: Build the widest ring first.** At the center layer, build a full 15-block circle (about 56 perimeter blocks) using the circle chart method. This is your equator.

**Step 3: Add the adjacent rings.** One layer above and one layer below the equator, build 13-block circles. Then 11, 7, 3, and finally 1-block caps at the very top and bottom.

**Step 4: Align the layers.** Every ring shares the same center column. Count from your marker block before placing each layer, and the sphere stays perfectly round.

**Step 5: Fill the interior.** A hollow sphere needs no fill — just keep the walls one block thick. For a solid sphere (like a statue base or a mining blob), fill every ring solid as you go, which runs about 700 blocks for a 15-block sphere.

**Step 6: Smooth the visible seams.** Walk around the finished sphere at eye level. If a diagonal step looks like a staircase, replace the offending block with a slab or stairs block to soften the silhouette.

Here are the layer-width patterns for the most requested diameters. Each list starts at the bottom of the sphere:

| Diameter | Layer widths (bottom to top) | Approximate surface blocks |

| 11 | 1, 3, 7, 9, 11, 11, 11, 9, 7, 3, 1 | 380 |
| 15 | 1, 3, 7, 11, 13, 15, 15, 15, 13, 11, 7, 3, 1 | 708 |
| 21 | 1, 3, 7, 11, 15, 17, 19, 21, 21, 21, 19, 17, 15, 11, 7, 3, 1 | 1,384 |

Odd diameters always end with a single-block cap, which makes the top of the sphere look sharp and clean. Even diameters end in a 2-block cap — a subtle difference that most players never notice.

## Floating Spheres and Lighting

The most dramatic spheres are floating ones. Build the sphere at its full size, then dig out the connecting pillar underneath, leaving a one-block column while you work. Two practical tips for suspended builds:

- **Light the interior.** Mob-proof the inside with sea lanterns or glowstone placed in the hollow before you close the top cap.
- **Add an access route.** A hidden staircase in the pillar, or an ender pearl landing pad on top, beats rebuilding the pillar every time you need to get inside.
