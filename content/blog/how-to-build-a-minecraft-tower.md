Every Minecraft base has that one empty corner begging for a tower. A good tower is also the perfect first test of your circle skills — it combines a circular foundation, curved walls, balconies, and a roof into one build you can finish in a single session. This guide walks through a 15-block-wide wizard tower from bare ground to pointed roof.

## Foundation: Pick Your Diameter and Clear the Site

The tower in this guide is **15 blocks wide**, which gives comfortable interior space without eating a mountain of blocks. Clear a 19×19 flat pad (4 blocks wider than the tower on every side) and mark the center with a temporary block.

Lay the 15-block base ring with the [circle generator](/?t=circle&d=15) blueprint in front of you — about 56 blocks around the perimeter. This ring defines both the wall line and the floor plan, so double-check its position before going vertical. Mark the four cardinal points (north, east, south, west) with a different block; they anchor every floor above.

## Walls: Build in Two-Ring Increments

Round walls are just stacked circle rings. Build up from the base ring, one full ring at a time, to the first floor height (6 blocks). Then pause and check: every ring must share the same center column, so sight down from the top marker block before placing ring two.

For a 15-block tower, keep the walls one block thick — 15 blocks of outer wall at each height is roughly 56 blocks per ring, and you will stack about 30 rings for a 30-block-tall tower, around 1,700 blocks total. That sounds like a lot until you realize the rings make the counting trivial: one ring, then the next, then the next.

**Window rhythm:** at every other level, replace one block with a glass pane on the cardinal points. A 15-wide ring has enough curve that a single pane reads as a window; two panes side by side are better on floors that get balconies.

## Balconies and Interiors

At height 18, add the first balcony. Place a 13-block ring *outside* the wall — the balcony is a ledge, not part of the tower — using slabs so you can walk around it. Build it as a full circle of slabs at the wall's outer edge, then remove the slab over the doorway and replace the doorway with a fence gate.

Inside, add floors every 6 blocks. The simplest interior trick: place a full 15-block disc of planks at each floor height (about 177 blocks), then cut a spiral staircase around the center column. The staircase circle is a 3-block-wide spiral with a 1-block gap in the middle — spiral staircases need no railings if the gap is over the same column on every level.

## Roof: Taper the Rings

The roof is a mini-dome with a point. From the top wall ring, stack shrinking rings — **13, 11, 7, 3, 1** — the same pattern as our [dome guide](/blog/how-to-build-a-dome-in-minecraft/), but pull each ring inward so the roof overhangs the walls slightly. Finish with a single block, then top it with a fence post and a lantern for the classic tower silhouette.

If you want a shorter, chunkier tower, stop the taper at the 7-block ring and cap it flat with a wall and parapet. Same walls, different crown.

## Lighting and the Finished Tower

Light the inside as you build: a sea lantern in the floor of every balcony room and one torch on the inside of every window wall. Towers are dark precisely because builders finish the shell and forget the interior — lighting as you go means no mob-clearing run at the end.

Stand back at night and admire it. The curve of the rings, the glow of the balcony lights, and the taper of the roof all come from the same skill: building one circle ring at a time. Practice on a 9-block watchtower first if this is your first tower, and keep the [circle generator](/?t=circle&d=15) open for every ring count.
